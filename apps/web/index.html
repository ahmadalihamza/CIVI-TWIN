Our approach

Distance should not decide who gets care

In mountainous districts, a long journey can stand between a family and a health visit. CIVITWIN helps planners see who is reached, who is left behind and what each rupee buys, so decisions about new services can be fairer and easier to explain to the people they affect.

Our principles

How we keep the tool useful, honest and humane

People decideThe model advises. A planner reviews every plan, confirms its exact hash and records their judgement before anything is shared.
Every number is checkedAn independent audit recomputes all constraints and metrics. No language model produces or changes a number.
Fairness shown, not claimedWe show the worst-served cluster, unmet demand and who is still far away — instead of a single "fair" score.
Privacy by designOnly aggregate, non-identifying planning data. No patient records, no individual tracking, runs fully offline.
Local languageAsk in English or Urdu and read the explanation in Urdu. Pashto is planned, with review by fluent speakers.
Honest about the dataToday's demo is synthetic and labelled everywhere. Real use needs approved aggregate data and local validation.

Who it is for

  • District health officers and planners comparing options for new services.
  • NGOs and development partners planning outreach, mobile clinics and health camps.
  • Community representatives, who can see where services go and why, in plain language.
  • Lady Health Worker supervisors, who know the roads, seasons and families behind the numbers.

What it is — and is not

It is
  • A scenario sandbox for planning access
  • Transparent, reproducible maths
  • A way to compare options before field checks
It is not
  • Medical advice or emergency dispatch
  • An approved government allocation
  • A statement about real district services

From demo to a real pilot

A conditional 90-day path — only with approval from the responsible authorities

Days 1–30 · Listen and validateAgree data sharing and governance; review aggregate demand, capacity and travel times with district staff and community voices.
Days 31–60 · CalibrateReplace synthetic inputs with approved data; compare model plans with planners' own options; test Urdu and Pashto wording.
Days 61–90 · Supervised trialsUse in real planning discussions with people in charge; record feedback; decide together whether to expand to Mohmand and Khyber.

How it works

Four steps, and a person signs off at the end

1 · DescribeType the request in English or Urdu, or pick a quick scenario. The form fills itself for you to check.
2 · Review & confirmSee every value and confirm the plan's fingerprint. Nothing runs before that.
3 · Solve & auditBaseline, a simple benchmark and the optimized plan, then 12 independent checks.
4 · Decide togetherRead who is reached and who is not, add your field notes, and share the report.

Why we built this

Decisions about where to open a new health unit are usually made in meetings, with spreadsheets and good intentions. We wanted something a district officer could open on an ordinary laptop, ask a question in their own language, and get an answer they could explain to a village elder: which families get closer to care, which don't, and what it costs.

Everything here runs offline and every number is checked twice. The data today is made up — the questions are real.
